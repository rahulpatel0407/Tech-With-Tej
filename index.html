01 — About

Why this work.

I got into engineering because I like systems that have to actually work — not demo well, work. My first real taste of that was at IOCL, watching refinery engineers make critical decisions off sensor dashboards I helped build. If anomaly detection missed something, that wasn't an abstract metric — it was an operational risk on a live refinery floor.

Cybersecurity followed naturally. It's the same instinct pointed at a different kind of signal: instead of catching a failing sensor, you're catching an intrusion before it becomes an incident. At NRSC (ISRO), I'm doing exactly that — building analytics for anomalous activity in a live, secure enterprise environment.

AI is the engine that makes both possible at scale. AgriHealth AI came from applying this exact approach where it mattered personally: smallholder farmers who lose an entire season to a disease they couldn't diagnose in time. Getting a model in front of people who could actually act on it proved how transformative applied AI can be when built for real-world constraints.

03 — Selected Work

Two systems, two kinds of stakes.

AgriHealth AI

IBM Hackathon 2026 Winner

A CNN-based crop-disease detector built for farmers with patchy connectivity — fine-tuned on domain-specific imagery, benchmarked for edge deployment, and pitched directly to Bihar's Principal Secretary (Agriculture).

10,000+Potential Users
↓35%Misclassification
<3sResponse Time
TensorFlow / PyTorchCNN Fine-tuningIoT Edge Inference

The Problem

Smallholder farmers often lose a season to a crop disease they catch too late — there's rarely an affordable way to diagnose a problem in the field, in real time, without reliable internet.

Research & Approach

Fine-tuned a CNN on domain-specific agricultural imagery rather than reaching for an off-the-shelf classifier. The ~35% reduction in misclassification came from systematic ablation studies across architecture configurations — testing what actually moved the number versus what was noise.

Architecture & Edge Inference

Since target users have inconsistent connectivity, the model couldn't assume a cloud round-trip. Compared edge vs. cloud inference latency across 4 IoT hardware configurations, optimizing for the setup that held end-to-end response under 3 seconds.

Challenges

The real tension was accuracy vs. latency vs. hardware cost — a more accurate model is worthless if it can't run on affordable edge hardware in the field. Every experiment was a response to that tension, balancing weight quantization against classification confidence.

Recognition & Impact

IBM Hackathon 2026 Winner. Presented at AI Summit Bihar 2026 to industry leaders and policymakers; formal proposal submitted to the DBT Cell. Received formal appreciation from Bihar's Education Minister and the Public Health Engineering Department Minister.

Lessons Learned

Deploying models to edge devices demonstrated that model accuracy is meaningless without hardware-conscious design. Latency and memory footprint constraints dictate architecture choices far more than theoretical benchmark scores.

What's Next

Expanding multimodal disease diagnosis, optimizing INT8 quantization on microcontrollers, and formalizing the pilot deployment pipeline with regional agricultural authorities.

PPE Detection Kit

Real-time Safety Compliance

A YOLOv8 computer-vision pipeline that flags missing safety gear on an industrial floor in under 200ms a frame.

<200msDetection Latency
91%+Precision
3Baselines Benchmarked
PyTorchOpenCVYOLOv8

The Problem

Manual PPE compliance checks on an industrial floor don't scale and don't catch violations in real time, exposing workers to avoidable occupational hazards.

Research & Approach

Built on YOLOv8 for real-time object detection, benchmarked against 3 baseline detection architectures to validate the choice before committing to production training pipelines.

Challenges

Holding sub-200ms latency while keeping precision above 91% meant tuning confidence thresholds, NMS parameters, and input resolution together, not independently — conducting systematic ablation to find the parameter sweet spot.

Lessons Learned

In real-time computer vision pipelines, end-to-end latency is driven by I/O and post-processing as much as inference. Tuning NMS thresholding and frame-skipping heuristics yielded higher FPS gains than pruning the model backbone.

What's Next

Extending detection to dynamic safety zone breaches, hazardous posture estimation, and edge RTSP stream ingestion for factory-wide CCTV monitoring.
